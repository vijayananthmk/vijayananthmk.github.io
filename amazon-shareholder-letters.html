Why You Should Read Amazon Shareholder Letters from 1997 to 2020

Notes on Jeff Bezos's shareholder letters

If one is interested in business, investing, and long-term thinking, one should read the Amazon shareholder letters by Jeff Bezos. I still remember Charlie Munger comparing Jeff Bezos with Lee Kuan Yew for his leadership qualities.

Firstly, Jeff's emphasis on long-term thinking — how he started small and made Amazon huge. Starting with the 1997 shareholder letter, he appended his original 1997 letter every year. His views from that year remained intact for the shareholders.

He stressed upon the words “It's All About the Long Term”, and that it is always Day 1.

His views on how a business should be run, and why we should focus on customers' problems and needs rather than being competitor-focused.

His differentiation between Day 1 and Day 2, and how Day 2 will look like, is another important lesson.

His lesson on decision-making. What are the two types of decisions? When should one act quickly, and when should one be slow in making a decision?

The one-way door and two-way door.

Today, many people are advocating for a longer attention span. Our attention span is getting shorter because of shorter forms of videos and information snacking.

But Jeff spoke about this way back in 2007, while launching Kindle, and wanted people to read.

In 2004, he talked about the important metric free cash flow. He said he would not focus on earnings alone. The two important metrics are return on invested capital and free cash flow.

His views about risk-taking are also worth understanding — how a few winners can pay for lots of losers and failed experiments.

His last shareholder letter was about being unique, being original, and what it takes to be original. The way he uses metaphors.

There are a lot of things you could learn from one of the world's great thinkers. I couldn't recall or write down many of the things here.

I have added the link to the Amazon shareholder letters in the Elsewhere section. Just have a read.
