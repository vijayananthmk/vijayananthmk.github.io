What Investing Has Taught Me So Far

August 16, 2026

I started investing because I wanted to make money.

Over time, I realised that investing teaches you much more than how to make money. It teaches you how to think.

1. Patience is a competitive advantage

The market constantly gives us reasons to act.

A stock goes up and we want to buy more.
A stock falls and we want to sell.
Someone else makes money and we feel like we are missing out.

But most of the time, doing nothing is harder than doing something.

Good businesses need time to grow. Compounding needs time to work. The ability to wait is therefore one of the most valuable skills an investor can develop.

2. Price and value are different things

A great company can be a bad investment if you pay too much for it.

And a company that looks cheap is not necessarily undervalued.

I have slowly started to understand that investing is not simply about finding good companies. It is about trying to understand what a business is worth and comparing that with what the market is asking me to pay.

3. I know much less than I think

Every year I learn something new about investing, and every year I discover how much I still don't know.

There are thousands of companies, industries, technologies and economic variables.

That has made me more comfortable saying:

I don't know.

Not knowing is not a weakness. Pretending to know is.

4. A good business is more than a good stock chart

A stock price tells me what the market is doing.

A business tells me why it might happen.

I increasingly find myself looking at things such as competitive advantages, capital allocation, management, cash flows, debt, returns on capital and the size of the opportunity.

The more I learn, the more interesting the business behind the ticker becomes.

5. Losing money teaches you differently

Reading about risk is very different from experiencing it.

When an investment falls, it forces you to ask questions that theory cannot answer:

Did I make a mistake?
Did my original thesis change?
Was I simply unlucky?
Would I buy the same investment today?

Those questions are often more valuable than the loss itself.

6. Compounding is not just about money

The idea of compounding has changed how I think about many things.

Money compounds.
Knowledge compounds.
Relationships compound.
Reputation compounds.

Small improvements repeated for years can produce results that are difficult to imagine at the beginning.

That may be one of the most important lessons investing has given me.

7. The goal is not to be right all the time

Investing has made me less interested in being right and more interested in making good decisions.

You can make a good decision and get a bad outcome. You can make a bad decision and get a good outcome.

The important thing is to build a process that works over a long period of time.

8. Investing has made me more curious

Perhaps this is the part I enjoy most.

An investment can lead me into an industry I know nothing about.

Then I start reading about the industry. Then I learn about its customers, competitors, technology and economics.

One question leads to another.

Investing has become less about watching prices and more about understanding the world.

And I think that's why I want to keep doing it for a very long time.

I don't know where my investing journey will take me.

But I know I want to keep learning, keep questioning my assumptions, and keep getting a little better at thinking.

That's what investing has taught me so far.
